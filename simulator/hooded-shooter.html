How the math works

Perfect grip. The hood doesn't move, so a ball pinched between the wheel and the hood rolls along the hood. Its center moves at the average of the two surfaces: v = v_wheel / 2, where v_wheel = ω_wheel · R_wheel. The spin is ω_ball = v_wheel / (2·r_ball). That makes the spin ratio r·ω / v = 1 for every perfect-grip hooded shot, whatever the wheel size or RPM.

Slip. The ball enters at the feed speed with no spin. Squeezing it by the compression c gives a normal force N_wheel = k·c. The hood pushes back with N_hood = N_wheel + m·v²/ρ, because the ball travels on a curve of radius ρ = R_wheel + r_ball − c. Friction at each contact is at most μ·N and opposes the sliding at that contact:

  • wheel contact slip: u_w = v_wheel − (v + ω·r)
  • hood contact slip: u_h = v − ω·r
  • m·dv/dt = F_w + F_h, I·dω/dt = r·(F_w − F_h), I_wheel·dΩ/dt = τ_motor − F_w·R_wheel

This is stepped forward a microsecond at a time until the ball has rolled through the hood's wrap angle. With enough friction and contact length, both slips reach zero and the ball locks into perfect grip. Otherwise it leaves slower and with less spin. Each shot also takes energy from the flywheel, so the wheel slows down. The motor fights back at full power, using the linear torque curve of a goBILDA 6000 RPM motor (stall torque 0.144 N·m). That slowdown, not slip, is usually why a real shot falls short of v_wheel/2, so more spinning mass makes shots more consistent.

Flight. The flight includes gravity, drag ½ρ·C_d·A·v² and Magnus lift ½ρ·C_L·A·v², with C_L = S/(2S+1) and S = r·ω/v. Backspin lifts the ball and topspin pushes it down. Spin decay is ignored because the flight is short.

Bouncing in the cell. Each cell wall is a thin line, and so are the two lips. When the ball touches one, the wall pushes back with a spring-damper force N = k·δ − c·v_n. Here δ is how far the ball has squished into the wall, k is the panel give and c is tuned so a straight-on hit keeps fraction e of its speed. Friction acts along the wall, up to μ·N. It pushes the contact point's slip u = v_along − ω·r toward zero, but never past it. When friction is strong enough the ball grips the wall for a moment, so its spin flips to match rolling along that wall. That's why backspin hitting the back wall can kick the ball down into the cell, while topspin can climb the wall and pop out. The ball scores if it comes to rest inside the cell. The HIVE tipping and the side walls aren't modeled.

Field geometry (Competition Manual §9, TU04, Fig. 9-10 and 9-13): the upward CELL opening runs from 53.5 in to 65.6 in above the tiles. The opening is 14 in, tilted 30° toward the shooter, and the cell is 12 in deep. The model assumes the upward cell faces you, and it scores a shot as IN when the ball crosses that opening with at least one ball radius of clearance at each lip. POLLEN is 2.8 in and 0.055 lb; NECTAR is 3.6 in and 0.091 lb (AndyMark). The flywheel defaults to goBILDA parts: the Hogback Traction Wheel (72 mm, 55 g, 50A silicone) and the Steel Flywheel (60 mm, 115 g, 551 g·cm²). The Hogback's MOI is estimated at 430 g·cm² because goBILDA doesn't publish it, and the 0.9 friction value is an estimate too.

Estimates you should calibrate: the friction values, ball/wheel stiffness, C_d and Magnus scale are reasonable starting points, not measurements. Shoot real balls, measure range and apex height, then adjust friction, stiffness and Magnus until the sim matches. After that it can predict.
